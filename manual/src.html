Safety and install

Snoop37 turns the SMK-37 into a stand-alone groovebox: three synth tracks and a drum machine, made and sequenced on the keyboard's own chip. It is SLOOP 2.7.1 by isod89, ported to the SMK-37 by genkigenki.

NOTE Unofficial firmware. Not made or supported by M-Vave. No warranty: you install it at your own risk.

Which models

SMK-37 Pro, SMK-37 Elite, MKE-P37 and Starrykey 37 Play are one keyboard under different names. The controls and this manual are the same for all of them. Each model has its own package only so that M-Vave's updater still knows the unit when you go back.

Install or update

Open genkigenki.github.io/smk37 in Chrome or Edge.
Connect the keyboard with a USB data cable, straight into the computer (no hub).
Press Connect, allow MIDI, then Install.
Do not unplug while the update screen is up. The keyboard restarts on the Snoop37 intro.

Updating later works the same way. Projects, presets, samples, kits and settings are kept. From a build folder you can also run python3 tools/fm1_install.py build/sloop-smk37-elite.fwsc (Python 3 with mido and python-rtmidi).

Back up first

Before an update or a rollback, save a backup in the web editor: Projects > Backup > Save a backup (see Rescue). For a full 1 MiB flash dump, use the chip's UBOOT mode: hold OCT− + OCT+ for 5 s while stopped, then a JieLi UBOOT tool such as jl-uboot-tool. Keep the dump private: it holds data unique to your unit.

NOTE With a UBOOT tool, never write 0xC0000–0xC1FFF (factory key and pad calibration, Bluetooth pairing), 0xFF000–0xFFFFF (key_mac) or 0xF4000–0xFDFFF (stock resources). They are unique to your unit. Snoop37 itself never writes them.

Back to the M-Vave firmware

Save a backup in the editor.
Optional: editor Settings > Prepare the return to M-Vave. It erases a marker so the stock firmware rebuilds its factory key and knob map (Snoop37 also does this by itself when it first stores a sample).
Install the official package for your model with M-Vave's M-UPGRADE, or with fm1_install.py --force SMK-37_Pro_016.fwsc. Close every other MIDI app first.

If it does not start

After 2 failed starts, Snoop37 opens its USB recovery screen: install again from the web page. After a third, the chip drops into UBOOT. You can also hold OCT− while you switch on: SNOOP37 USB RESCUE.

The three synth tracks share 8 voices. The drum track has 16 sounds and 6 voices of its own. K3 (track) picks the track you play, record and edit, on every screen. The screen takes the colour of that track. White always means "what you touch". Red always means "recording".

Tap and hold

Every function button has two jobs.

TAP: its pages open (sound design, settings). Tap again: the next page. K2 or < > also walk the pages. Each button remembers its last page.
HOLD: a layer. While it is held, the 16 white keys, the pads and the four dials do something else. After 0.14 s the screen shows 16 tiles. Let go: back to playing.

The layers are FX punch-in, PARA erase, ARP roll, SEQ steps, SCALE key, GLOBE mix and BT song. CHORD and NOTE REPEAT only open pages.

Inside any layer: PLAY still starts and stops, K2 is always the tempo, and K3 / K4 wait (no jump when you let go).

Lock a layer

Hold the layer button and tap PATCH. The layer stays open with the button let go (LOCK on the screen, the button blinks). Both hands are free. Any other button closes it and does only that. SEQ PLAY, SEQ REC and OCTAVE−/+ still work inside a locked layer.

Tiles on the pads

In the SEQ, FX, GLOBE and BT layers, pad n = white key n: top row 1–8, bottom row 9–16.

GO values

A value that does something (LOAD, SAVE, ERASE, NEW, CLRSQ, INIT) needs two clicks: turn its dial one click, AGAIN: SAVE shows, turn one more click within about 1.5 s.

START > FIRST BEAT

Your first beat in 60 seconds

Turn K3 to track 4 (orange). Turn K4 to pick a kit: try 808 or BOOMBAP. The pads already play the drums.
Press SEQ REC (rec ready). Play a beat freely on the pads or keys, at your own tempo. No click, no count-in. Hold OCTAVE− for ghost notes, OCTAVE+ for hard hits.
Press SEQ REC on the "1" after your last bar. The loop closes: its length sets the tempo, the hits snap to the grid, and it plays.
Press SEQ REC again while it plays to record on top. Hold ARP and hold the hat: a 1/16 hat roll.
Turn K3 to track 1 (blue, 808 BOOM), press SEQ REC, play a bass line on the keys.
Hold SCALE and press the key of your song (for example D). Select track 2, hold SCALE and turn DIAL 1 to 7TH: every white key now plays a chord of the key.
Hold FX and hold a key for a punch-in effect. Still holding FX, turn DIAL 2 for DUST, DIAL 3 for DUCK.
A mistake? PARA + OCTAVE−: undo.
Keep it: hold BT and press key 5 (pad 5): the loop is saved as section A.

Keys and velocity

Synth tracks (1–3)

All 37 keys play the selected track. The notes always go out on USB MIDI (and the TRS jack) on the track's channel. OCTAVE− / OCTAVE+: octave −3 … +3; both together: 0.

The GM KIT sound is the exception: the lowest key is a kick, no scale.

Drum track (4)

The 16 white keys F3–G5 play the 16 drum sounds (Drum track). A black key plays the sound of the white key on its left: two fingers on one sound for fast rolls. The outer keys add C3 kick, D3 snare, E3 hat, G#5 cowbell, A5 open hat, B5 crash, C6 ride. Hold OCTAVE− for ghost hits, OCTAVE+ for hard hits.

Key velocity

HOME menu (hold PATCH) > PLAY > KEY VELOCITY (K5) sets how the 37 keys and the 16 pads play. It changes the notes, their MIDI out and the drum levels. OCTAVE−/+ held still play ghost / hard drums.

Drum levels and accents

Every drum hit has one of four levels: GHOST, SOFT, NORM, HARD. They come from the velocity, or from OCTAVE−/+ while you play, or later from the step sequencer. On synth tracks a step can carry a velocity (1–127) and an accent.

PLAY > PADS

The 16 pads

The pads have velocity and RGB light. What they do depends on the screen and on the layer you hold.

Drum kit (default)

DRUM KIT: the bottom row is drum sounds 1–8 (keys F3–F4), the top row sounds 9–16 (keys G4–G5). Low sounds sit at the bottom.

On the PLAY screen each pad plays a drum sound with velocity, whatever track is selected. So the keys play a synth and the pads play the drums. While you record, pad hits go into the drum track. Pads are orange: dim, bright while they sound.

Steps

STEPS: set steps in the track colour, empty steps dim white, the playhead white.

Press KNOB BANK + FADER BANK to switch the pads to STEPS, and again to go back. The pads show the selected track's 16 steps of the current page in reading order (top row 1–8, bottom row 9–16). Tap a pad to set or clear a step. On the drum track a new step uses the sound you played last. < > change the step page (1–16, 17–32…).

MAKE > RECORDING

Recording

Snoop37 records live and layers every pass on top of the last (overdub). Notes go to the nearest step as you heard them: the ~12 ms between a key and its sound are taken back. Chords are kept on POLY tracks (up to 4 notes a step); MONO, LEGATO and UNISON sounds record one note at a time. Held notes become ties.

What SEQ REC does

REC again while armed disarms it. While recording, the REC light is solid and the track shows a red rec. Turn K3 while recording and the take moves to the next track without stopping. In song mode, REC waits until the song is stopped.

The REC screen (armed, before the first note)

  • mode: free = a free take, the tempo follows you · tempo = record at the tempo set (K2).
  • start: note = your first note starts the loop · count = press PLAY, one bar of clicks (4 3 2 1), then recording starts.
  • quant off: a note still goes into the nearest step, and how far off the grid you played it becomes the step's nudge. The pattern keeps your groove and stays editable.

During a count-in, REC cancels it and PLAY goes back to rec ready. K2 sets the tempo on this screen.

Free take

On an empty project, press REC and play freely. The screen shows the seconds and the loop it would make now (2 bars · 92 bpm).
Press REC on the "1" after your last bar. Snoop37 picks 1, 2 or 4 bars at the nearest tempo, writes your notes and plays the loop. All four tracks take that length.
PLAY during a free take drops it. A take closes by itself after 24 s.

Metronome and swing

  • The PLAY light flashes on every beat: a silent metronome.
  • Audible click: GLO > GLOBAL > CLICK = OFF, REC (only while recording) or ON. Its level follows GLO > DRUMS > LVL. It is never recorded. The count-in always clicks.
  • Swing 0–100: TRACKS screen DIAL 1 or GLO > GLOBAL > SWING for all tracks; SEQ + DIAL 3 per track (adds to the global). Triplets are never swung.

What gets recorded

  • Synth tracks: notes and chords with their lengths, ties, ARP rolls as ratchets, CHORD+ chords as they sound.
  • Drum track: hits with their levels, ARP rolls as ratchets. Pad hits on the DRUM KIT pads too.
  • Never recorded: punch-in effects, the click, notes played in a layer that uses the keys (FX, SEQ, GLOBE…).

Over held notes

On a POLY track, a note you record while held notes of the pattern still ring no longer cuts them. Up to 4 notes sound at once on a track. Entering steps by hand (SEQ + key) still replaces what plays.

With a MIDI keyboard

REC records the selected track. A keyboard on track 1, 2 or 3's channel always plays that track. Set it to channel 4 (or any free channel) to play and record the selected track. If your keyboard plays another track, the screen says MIDI PLAYS TRACK n.

MAKE > STEP SEQUENCER

Steps, nudge, locks, fills

Each track has up to 64 steps with its own length (1–64) and division. Tracks of different lengths loop on their own and stay in phase (polymeters). A synth step holds up to 4 notes with a level and ratchet, a tie or rest, an accent and a slide. A drum step holds any of the 16 sounds, each with its own level and ratchet.

Three ways to enter steps: live (REC and play), the SEQ layer (hold SEQ), and the STEP page (tap SEQ, synth tracks). On the SMK-37 there is a fourth: the pads on STEPS.

STEP page (tap SEQ)

  • Keys: the keys pressed together become the cursor's step (POLY: up to 4 notes). Let go of all keys and the cursor moves on. A MIDI keyboard works too.
  • LEN on a note: how many steps it lasts. Longer turns the next steps into TIEs (stops at the next note: NOTE AHEAD, END OF PATTERN).
  • FLAG: ACC accent, SLD slide to the next note, A+S both.
  • PARA tapped: clears the step and moves on.

Not while recording or armed. On the drum track the page says DRUM TRACK: use the DRUMS grid or the SEQ layer.

PATTERN page (tap SEQ again)

DIV: 1/4, 1/8, 1/16 (default), 1/32, 8T, 16T, 1/2, 1BAR, 2BAR. A 64-step track at 2BAR lasts 128 bars.

Nudge

SEQ + step + DIAL 4 moves a step off the grid, −32 … +31 in 1/64 of a step (minus = early, plus = late). Its ratchets move with it. On the drum track the whole step moves. A nudged step shows a dot in its tile.

Parameter locks

A lock gives one sound parameter another value for one step only.

Turn the parameter you want on its page (for example FX > DLY). It becomes the lock parameter (before you turn anything: ENV DEST > FLT).
Hold SEQ and hold the step.
Turn K4: the first click makes the lock, the next ones move it. The title line shows it (lock dst 14).
K3 (step still held) steps through the other parameters.

Up to 24 locks per track, one per parameter per step. Not lockable: arp, key / scale / chord, STRUM / VLEAD, voice mode, MUTE, LEN / DIV / SWG and global pages (NOT LOCKABLE, NO LOCK LEFT). SEQ + step + OCTAVE− clears locks and nudge. Undo does not bring locks back.

Page notes

  • Global pages (DLY, REV/CHO, GLOBAL, MASTER, SYSTEM, DRUMS) are the same whatever track is selected.
  • Drum track: SLICER, PATTERN, SONG and the global pages work. Its kit, level, reverb and pan are on the DRUMS kit page; its filter is FX + DIAL 4 or fader bank B.
  • FM6 tracks ignore ENV and ENV DEST (each operator has its own envelope).
  • ENV SYNC (ENV DEST > ESYNC ON): ATK, DEC, REL become note values of the tempo (0, 1/64T … 4BAR).
  • LFO 2: SYNC locks the cycle to the tempo (RATE ignored). TRIG NOTE restarts it on each phrase, FREE lets it run. POL UNI swings only above the value.
  • ARP modes (with C E G held): UP C E G · DN G E C · UPDN C E G E · DNUP G E C E · RND · ORD the order pressed · REPEAT the last note · UP+8 C E G C' · CONV C G E · DIVG E G C · PINKY C G E G · THUMB C E C G · WALK random steps · CHORD all held notes together. MODE OFF switches the arp off.
  • KBD HOLD: ARP 2 HOLD ON with ARP MODE OFF holds the notes you play, without an arpeggio. The next note with no key down replaces them; notes played while keys are down join them.
  • ARP SWG also works with the sequencer stopped. A running arp keeps going when you load another sound.
  • EDIT 1 / EDIT 2 carry the engine's own page names: ANALOG OSC / FLT, DIGITAL OPS / MOD, PHASE PHS / LINE, LOFI CHIP / MOTN, SAMPLE SET / TONE, VOICE VOWL / TONE, TRIO OSC / TONE, WHEEL BARS / TONE, GRAIN GRAN / SPRY, FM6 OPS / PATCH, PHYS BODY / EXCT, NOISE SRC / MOVE.

Where MIDI goes

  • USB: in and out, to a computer, phone or USB MIDI host.
  • TRS jack: on the SMK-37 it is a MIDI OUT only (no MIDI IN). It sends what goes out on USB: keys and pads, and the sequencer with OUT = SEQ, or DAW mode. Use a TRS-to-DIN adapter, type A; if a synth does not respond, try type B. Switch it with HOME menu > MIDI > TRS MIDI OUT.

GLO > SYSTEM

SETUP > DAW MODE

DAW mode

Press < + > (or HOME menu > MIDI > MODE) to switch SYNTH ↔ DAW. In DAW mode the keyboard is a MIDI controller on USB (and on the TRS jack with TRS MIDI OUT on). The sequencer stops and the controls leave Snoop37 alone. MIDI IN still plays Snoop37 as a sound module.

  • Keys: notes on CHANNEL (default 1). The lowest key C3 = note 48 at octave 0. OCTAVE−/+: octave ±3; both: 0.
  • K1–K8 (as printed), FADER 1–4, 16 pads, 13 buttons and wheels send what the map says. A knob, fader or wheel sends at most every 8 ms.
  • KNOB BANK / FADER BANK: bank A / B of the knobs / faders (lit on B). Both together: the pads' bank.
  • PATCH tapped: LOCAL ON / OFF (lit: ON). ON: the keys also play the selected track and NOTE pads the drum kit.
  • Screen: DAW, channel, LOCAL, the last control touched with its message and value, the banks, the octave, the eight knobs as bars.
  • Pads: NOTE bank A blue, bank B green; CC yellow; OFF faint.

SETUP > USB AUDIO

USB audio

On USB the keyboard is also an audio input named Felucca: 44.1 or 48 kHz, 16-bit stereo, no driver. Pick it in your DAW, Audacity or a phone app and record the master output. MIDI, the editor and the installer keep working on the same cable.

HOME menu > AUDIO > USB AUDIOMASTER (default): follows K1, keep K1 up · FULL: a fixed level, K1 only sets the headphones
HOME menu > AUDIO > USB RATEAUTO (default) / 44.1 KHZ (try this if a phone records it noisy) / 48 KHZ. Takes effect after you switch off and on (RESTART)

The first time, the computer sees a new device (MIDI + audio) and sets it up again.

SETUP > SAMPLES AND KITS

Your own samples and kits

Four sample slots, USR1–USR4, about 7.4 s each (22 kHz mono). A slot holds an instrument (synth track: engine SAMPLE, SET = USRn, or GRAIN SRC) or a drum kit (KIT = USRn, or USR3+4 for one big 15 s kit). You fill them from the web editor.

NOTE USR1 / USR2 are where the M-Vave firmware keeps its key and knob map. The first sample you store there makes the stock firmware rebuild its factory map if you go back.

An instrument (editor > Samples)

Choose a slot. The page warns before it replaces something.
Drop up to 16 WAV files (one note each; the note is read from the name, like KEYS_C4.wav), or Chop a recording into up to 16 pieces. Each file has Gain, Match levels, and Loop: ONE SHOT, LOOP or LOOP+TAIL with loop points.
Name it, Send to USRn, then Play this slot on track 1, 2 or 3.

CHOP: TAP (or space) while it plays to cut, Find hits, Grid, Equal parts; keep or drop chops; Fit to slot; Download WAVs; keys 1–0 to listen; double-click / drag / arrows / Del for markers; wheel to zoom.

A drum kit (editor > Drum kit)

Drop a WAV on each of the 16 pads, or Choose files (sorted by name: kick, snare, hh…). Each pad has Pitch, Gain, Length. Match levels evens them out.
Pick where it goes: USR3+4 (about 15 s) or one slot.
Name it, Send, then Use on the drum track. Save as ZIP / Open ZIP to share.

Synthesised kits SYN1–SYN4 (editor > Drum synth)

Pick a kit, name it (8 letters), start from any of the 32 kits. Pick a sound and change Tone (WAVE, PITCH, BEND, HOLD, DECAY, 2ND, CLICK), Noise (NOISE, CLAP, HPF, CHIP), Filter (type, cutoff, res, env) and Out (DRIVE, LEVEL). CRUSH and S&H act on the whole kit. Store on the device keeps the four kits.

SETUP > RESCUE

Backup, rescue, troubleshooting

Backup and restore

Editor > Projects > Backup. Save a backup writes everything to one file: the working loop, sections A–D, 32 user presets, the FM6 bank, SYN kits, samples USR1–USR4 and settings. Restore from a file puts it all back (stop the song first). A damaged file is refused before anything is written. Save a backup before every update.
